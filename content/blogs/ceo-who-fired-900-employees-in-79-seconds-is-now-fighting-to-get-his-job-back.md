# CEO Who Fired 900 Employees in 79 Seconds Is Now Fighting to Get His Job Back

**19 August 2026 · Technology Business · Corporate News**

A few years ago, Vishal Garg became one of the most talked-about CEOs in the business world after telling around **900 employees they were being laid off during a video call that lasted just 79 seconds**.

Now, years later, Garg is at the centre of another corporate battle.

Better Home & Finance, the US mortgage technology company formerly known as Better.com, has asked a federal court to restrict its former CEO from seeking support from the company's shareholders for at least 30 days. The company alleges that Garg has been trying to rally investors to help him return to the CEO position and has made statements that Better says violate federal securities laws.

The dispute is a remarkable turn in the story of a CEO whose management decisions had already attracted intense public attention.

## Quick Summary

Better Home & Finance says former CEO Vishal Garg was removed from his position on August 3, 2026, after the company's board cited large cumulative losses and a steep decline in its stock price during his tenure.

Garg is now trying to regain support from shareholders. The company wants a court to stop that effort temporarily and invalidate any shareholder approval he may already have obtained.

At the centre of the dispute is a much bigger question: **who should control the future direction of the company — the current board or its former CEO?**

## Why Better Home Wants Vishal Garg Blocked

According to the company's court filing, Better Home's board voted unanimously, excluding Garg, to remove him as CEO on **August 3**.

The company pointed to more than **$1.5 billion in net losses since 2022** and said its stock price had fallen by more than **90% during Garg's time as CEO**.

After being removed, Garg did not simply walk away.

Better Home says he began trying to build shareholder support for his return.

On August 10, Garg reportedly sent a letter to the company's board calling for the immediate resignation of all board members. He also said he had created a group of concerned shareholders that could support an effort to bring him back as CEO.

The company alleges that his campaign included press releases, text messages, social media activity and an interview with Bloomberg.

Better Home is now asking the court to restrict Garg from contacting shareholders for at least 30 days and to invalidate any shareholder support or approval it says he may already have gathered.

These are allegations made in litigation, not findings that Garg has violated the law.

## Vishal Garg Wants His Old Job Back

Garg has made it clear that he does not agree with the decision to remove him.

According to the report, he has asked the board to reverse the decision and offered to continue as CEO for **$1 a year** until the company returns to profitability, after which he says he would step down.

His position is that the company still has a path forward.

Garg has also said that investors contacted him after his removal and wanted him to return.

That creates an unusual corporate situation.

The board believes a leadership change is necessary.

The former CEO believes shareholders should have a say in reversing that decision.

And now the courts have become part of the fight.

## The 79-Second Layoff Call That Made Garg Famous

The current dispute is impossible to separate from the event that made Garg internationally known.

In **December 2021**, Garg informed approximately 900 Better employees that they were being dismissed during a video call that lasted about **79 seconds**.

The episode attracted widespread criticism because of both the size of the layoffs and the extremely brief way the announcement was delivered.

Garg later described the dismissed employees in harsh terms, adding further controversy around the incident. The company subsequently placed him on leave, although he was later reinstated as CEO in 2022.

That history still follows Garg today.

What began as a controversial management decision became part of his public reputation as an executive.

## Why This Story Matters Beyond One CEO

At first glance, this may look like another disagreement between a former executive and a company's board.

But there is a larger issue underneath it: **corporate control**.

When a CEO leaves a company, the board normally has significant authority over leadership decisions. Shareholders, however, also have important rights, particularly when governance disputes become serious.

That creates a complicated balance.

A board may argue that a former executive should no longer influence management.

The former executive may argue that shareholders have the right to decide whether the board made the correct choice.

That tension can become especially complicated when the former CEO remains a significant shareholder or has relationships with other investors.

## What Better Home Is Alleging in Court

Better Home's complaint reportedly claims Garg said he had already secured support representing **52% of the shareholder vote**.

The company says he made that claim without filing the required proxy statements with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

A proxy is essentially a formal mechanism through which shareholders can authorize someone to vote on their behalf in corporate matters.

Because shareholder voting involves securities laws and regulatory requirements, disputes over solicitation and proxy materials can become legally significant very quickly.

It is important to distinguish between an allegation and a proven violation.

At this stage, the reported claims are part of Better Home's case before the court.

## Garg's Argument for a Return

Garg's argument is more straightforward.

He appears to believe that removing him could hurt the company's recovery rather than help it.

He has acknowledged that execution during his leadership was not perfect while continuing to argue that Better Home's future remains promising.

His willingness to work for a symbolic salary of $1 could also be viewed as an attempt to convince shareholders that he is focused on rebuilding the company rather than personal compensation.

Whether investors agree is another question entirely.

Ultimately, shareholders may have to weigh the company's past performance against their view of who is most capable of leading its next phase.

## A Company Searching for a Turnaround

Better Home operates in the mortgage technology space, where business conditions can be heavily influenced by interest rates, housing activity, financing demand and broader economic conditions.

That makes leadership decisions particularly important.

A company attempting to recover from large losses has to answer several difficult questions:

### Can the business become profitable?

A new CEO may be expected to improve operations and restore financial discipline.

### Does the company need continuity?

A former CEO may argue that returning leadership can protect strategic plans already underway.

### What do shareholders believe?

Ultimately, investors will judge the competing arguments through shareholder votes, market reactions and other governance mechanisms.

## Why the Court's Decision Could Matter

Better Home is not asking the court merely to settle a disagreement over personalities.

It wants temporary restrictions on Garg's ability to communicate with shareholders and wants any shareholder approval he may already have collected to be invalidated, according to the reported complaint.

That means the court's response could influence how quickly the shareholder campaign develops.

A restriction could give the company's current leadership more breathing room.

Without one, Garg could have more opportunity to build support for his return.

The legal process therefore has potential consequences for both sides.

## What Happens Next?

The next stage will depend on the court's response to Better Home's request and on whether Garg continues his effort to regain shareholder support.

There are several possible outcomes.

The court could grant some or all of the company's requested restrictions.

It could reject the request.

Or the dispute could continue through additional legal proceedings while the shareholder battle develops separately.

At the same time, investors will be watching the company's financial performance.

Ultimately, corporate arguments are much easier to win when a leadership team can demonstrate improving results.

## The Bigger Lesson About Leadership

The most striking part of this story is how long the consequences of a leadership decision can last.

The 79-second layoff call happened years ago.

Yet it remains one of the defining moments in Garg's public career and continues to shape how many people view his leadership.

That does not, by itself, determine whether he can successfully run a company today.

Executives can change.

Companies can recover.

Strategies can fail and be rebuilt.

But once a leadership event becomes part of public memory, it can become much harder to separate the executive from the reputation created by that event.

## Did You Know?

Vishal Garg was later reinstated as CEO after the 2021 mass-layoff controversy, before eventually being removed again in 2026.

That makes the current dispute more than a simple CEO replacement.

It is another chapter in an unusually long-running battle over the direction of the company.

## Final Thought

Few executive stories come full circle as dramatically as this one.

A CEO once became globally known for ending hundreds of jobs in a video call lasting little more than a minute.

Years later, that same executive is fighting a very different battle — this time for his own job.

Whether Vishal Garg returns to Better Home & Finance will ultimately depend on the legal process, shareholder support and the company's future performance.

For now, the courtroom and the boardroom are both part of the same question:

**Who gets to decide what happens next?**
